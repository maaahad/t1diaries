InRange
MVP plan · 8 weeks · 1 developer · Rust + Next.js

A publishing and reference platform for the type 1 diabetes community: stories from parents and people living with T1D, a filtered feed of device and research news, a device reference, and the epidemiology in charts you can actually interrogate.

The plan, as a trace

Eight one-week sprints. The line is weekly delivery risk; the green corridor is the range a solo developer can hold at roughly 30 focused hours a week. Two weeks run above range — the ingestion engine and the statistics pipeline. Everything else is scoped to give those two room. Select a week to jump to it.

The four surfaces

Each one has to stand alone. If a visitor only ever uses one of these, it still has to be worth the visit.

Stories

Long-form writing by parents, adults with T1D, teens and caregivers. Markdown editor, drafts, tags by audience and topic, content warnings, comments, reading lists.

Signal

An aggregated, de-duplicated, categorised feed of device news, regulatory filings, trials and papers — with a human editorial gate before anything is published.

Devices

A structured reference for CGMs, pumps, AID systems and pens: specs, regional availability, compatibility, every field stamped with a source and a verification date.

Numbers

Interactive epidemiology — incidence, prevalence, technology uptake, outcomes by registry — each chart carrying its dataset version, licence and caveats.

Who it is for

Three readers drive every scoping decision. Anything that serves none of them is out.

A parent, four weeks after diagnosis

Wants to know what other parents did, what a closed loop actually is, and whether the thing they read at 2 a.m. is real. Needs plain language and a clear line between story and evidence.

An adult with T1D choosing hardware

Wants to compare a pump against their CGM and phone, know what is approved in their country, and follow what's coming. Needs specs with sources, not marketing copy.

An informed follower of the field

Clinician, researcher, advocate or the extremely online patient. Wants trials, preprints, filings and registry data without wading through supplement ads.

Not in the MVP audience: clinicians managing patients in-app, and anyone expecting live glucose data.

Boundaries

Explicit non-goals

Written down so they can be pointed at when week six gets tempting. Each of these is a plausible next quarter, not this one.

No health data

No CGM or pump uploads, no Nightscout sync, no glucose or dosing records. This keeps the platform out of medical-device and health-data territory for the MVP.

No dosing guidance

No calculators, correction factors or basal suggestions. Any user content drifting that way triggers a safety interstitial.

No native app

Responsive web only. A PWA manifest is the ceiling.

No forums or DMs

Comments on stories only. Private messaging multiplies the moderation surface far beyond one person's capacity.

No i18n

English only, but content and URL schemas are designed so locales can be added without a migration.

No payments

No subscriptions, donations or ads. Revenue is a post-launch conversation.

No trained models

Classification is rules plus embeddings plus an LLM call behind an editor's approval. Nothing bespoke is trained.

No full-text reproduction

Aggregated items store a headline, a short excerpt and a canonical link. Never the article body.

No microservices

One Rust binary with two run modes: API server and ingestion worker. One Postgres.

Feature by feature

What each module does

Eight modules. Each feature carries an ID used throughout the backend spec, a priority, and the sprint it lands in. Acceptance criteria are written as the checks you run before calling it done.

M1

Accounts & identity

Week 1

F-01Email and password sign-up Must

Argon2id hashing, minimum-length policy with a breach-list check, no security questions, no forced rotation.

  • Password never logged, never in a GraphQL error
  • Timing-equal response for existing vs unknown email
  • Signup rate-limited per IP and per email domain

F-02Email verification and password reset Must

Single-use tokens with a 30-minute expiry, sent through a transactional provider. Unverified accounts can read but not publish.

  • Token invalidated on use and on password change
  • Reset does not reveal whether the address exists

F-03Sessions and device list Must

Server-side sessions in a signed, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie. Users see active sessions and can end all of them.

  • Session rotates on privilege change
  • "Sign out everywhere" kills tokens within one request cycle

F-04Public profile Must

Handle, display name, avatar, short bio, self-declared connection to T1D (living with, parent, partner, caregiver, clinician, researcher), optional diagnosis year, optional country.

  • Every optional field defaults to hidden
  • Handle is immutable after first publish, display name is not

F-05Pseudonymity controls Must

A person can write under a handle with no other identifying field visible. Diagnosis year and country are opt-in per field, not per profile.

F-06Export and delete account Must

JSON export of profile, stories, comments and saved items. Deletion anonymises authored content by default, with a hard-delete option.

  • Export delivered within the session, not by email
  • Deletion completes in under 30 days and is logged

F-07Roles Must

Reader, author, editor, admin. Roles gate GraphQL fields through guards, not through the frontend.

F-08Social sign-in Post-MVP

Google and Apple. Deferred: adds an account-linking edge case set that is not worth a week-one day.

M2

Stories

Week 2, comments in Week 7

F-10Markdown editor with autosave Must

A plain writing surface with live preview, autosave every few seconds to a draft revision, and recovery after a crash. Restricted Markdown: no raw HTML, no scripts, no iframes except an allowlist.

  • Draft survives a hard browser kill
  • Sanitiser runs server-side on render, not only on save

F-11Images Must

Cover image and inline images to S3-compatible storage. Server-side resize to three widths, WebP output, EXIF stripped including GPS.

  • Uploads capped by size and MIME sniffed, not trusted by extension
  • Alt text required before publish

F-12Publish, unpublish, slug Must

Stable slug reserved at first publish, redirect kept if the title changes. Reading time computed at publish.

F-13Taxonomy Must

Three axes: audience (newly diagnosed, parent, teen, adult, partner), topic (school, sleep, travel, burnout, tech, insurance, exercise, food), and referenced devices. Curated vocabulary, not free tags.

  • Every story needs at least one audience and one topic
  • Device tags resolve to real device records, no orphans

F-14Content warnings Must

Author-set flags for DKA, hospitalisation, disordered eating, diabetes distress and bereavement. Flagged stories show an interstitial with a skip link and, where relevant, support signposting.

  • Interstitial is server-rendered, so it survives with JS off
  • Flag also suppresses the story from the front-page carousel

F-15Series Should

Ordered multi-part stories with previous/next navigation. Cheap to add once slugs exist.

F-16"This helped me" Should

One reaction type only, deliberately. No downvote, no like count race. Sorted feeds use it as a weak signal.

F-17Comments Should

One level of threading, Markdown-lite, editable for 15 minutes, author can close comments on their own story.

  • New accounts throttled to a low daily comment count
  • Links in comments limited and nofollow

F-18Report content Must

Any reader can report a story or comment with a reason code. Reports land in the moderation queue with the reported content snapshotted.

F-19Reading list Should

Save stories, news items and devices into one list with a filter by type.

F-20Author dashboard Could

Views, reactions and comments per story. Aggregate counts only — no per-reader analytics, ever.

F-21Outbound RSS Should

Full-content feed for stories, summary feed for Signal. Cheap, and it makes the platform a good citizen of the ecosystem it aggregates from.

M3

Signal — news aggregator

Weeks 3–4

F-30Source registry Must

Every source is a database row: kind (RSS, Atom, JSON Feed, REST API, GitHub releases, scraped page), endpoint, poll cadence, trust tier, licence note, default category, active flag. Sources are added through an admin form, never in code.

  • Adding a working source takes under two minutes with no deploy
  • Disabling a source stops fetches within one scheduler tick

F-31Polite fetcher Must

Conditional GET with ETag and Last-Modified, per-host concurrency limit, exponential backoff on failure, a descriptive User-Agent with a contact URL, and robots.txt respected for anything scraped.

  • Unchanged feeds cost a 304 and no parsing
  • Five consecutive failures auto-pause the source and alert

F-32Adapters Must

A common trait with implementations for generic feeds plus purpose-built adapters for PubMed, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA, Crossref and GitHub releases. Each adapter emits the same normalised item.

F-33Canonicalisation and dedup Must

Strip tracking parameters, resolve redirects, prefer DOI or PMID or NCT ID as the identity key, and fall back to a SimHash over title plus lede. The same trial covered by six outlets becomes one item with six mentions.

  • A story syndicated to three sites produces one card
  • Merging is reversible from the admin console

F-34Classification Must

Nine categories: CGM, pumps, AID and algorithms, insulin and drugs, cell and immune therapy, screening and prevention, access and policy, open source and DIY, data and apps. Keyword rules first, embeddings for the ambiguous remainder, editor confirms.

F-35Entity tagging Should

Link items to manufacturers, devices, trial registry IDs and journals so a device page can show its own news.

F-36Editorial queue Must

Nothing reaches the public feed unreviewed. Editors approve, rewrite the two-sentence summary, retag, pin or bin. Bulk actions and keyboard shortcuts, because this is a daily chore.

  • Triaging 50 items takes under ten minutes
  • Every action is written to an audit log with the actor

F-37Feed UI Must

Cursor-paginated list with filters by category, source type and date, plus a research-only toggle. Server-rendered first page for SEO.

F-38Item page Must

Headline, editor's summary, source and date, tags, other outlets covering the same thing, and a prominent link out to the original.

  • Stored excerpt never exceeds 240 characters
  • Canonical link is a plain outbound anchor, not a framed reader

F-39Evidence badges Should

For research items: study design, participant count, phase, registry identifier, and whether it is peer-reviewed, a preprint, a conference abstract or a press release. This is the single most useful thing the product can do for a frightened parent.

F-40Weekly digest Should

One email, Thursday, top items by category plus one story. Double opt-in, one-click unsubscribe.

F-41Saved items Should

Shares the reading list from F-19.

F-42Community submissions Could

Readers submit a URL; it enters the same editorial queue marked as user-submitted.

F-43Source health dashboard Must

Per source: last successful fetch, items in the last 30 days, error rate, average latency, and current backoff state. Without this the pipeline rots invisibly.

M4

Devices

Week 5

F-50Device directory Must

CGMs, insulin pumps, AID systems, smart pens and companion apps. Target 40–60 records at launch, entered by hand — this is content work, not a scraping problem.

F-51Structured specs Must

Typed per category: wear duration, calibration requirement, published accuracy claim, approved age range, water rating, reservoir volume, tubing, control algorithm, target adjustability, phone OS support, data-sharing options.

  • Every spec field is nullable and carries its own source and date
  • Unknown renders as "not published", never as a blank or a zero

F-52Compare up to three Must

Side-by-side table with differences highlighted and a shareable URL encoding the selection.

F-53Compatibility matrix Should

Which pump works with which sensor under which algorithm, on which phone, in which region. Modelled as explicit compatibility edges with a status of supported, announced, unsupported or discontinued.

F-54Regional availability Should

Per-country market status and regulatory route, kept deliberately coarse: available, approved but not launched, in review, not available.

F-55Related news and stories Should

Device pages pull in tagged Signal items and stories, which is what makes the directory worth revisiting.

F-56Verification stamp Must

Every record shows when it was last verified and by whom. Records past 180 days display a staleness notice.

F-57Correction requests Should

A form on every device page routing into the moderation queue with the field and the proposed source.

M5

Numbers — interactive statistics

Week 6

F-60Dataset registry Must

Each dataset row records publisher, version or edition, retrieval date, licence, permitted use and the transform script that produced the fact table. No chart may reference a dataset without one.

F-61ETL into fact tables Must

Idempotent, re-runnable jobs that land raw files, normalise country codes to ISO 3166-1 alpha-3, harmonise age bands, and write a single long-format observation table.

  • Re-running a job produces an identical table
  • Every observation row keeps its dataset and source row reference

F-62Incidence and prevalence explorer Must

Filter by country, year range and age band; switch between counts and rates per 100,000; toggle children-only.

F-63World map Must

Choropleth with a year scrubber and a country drill-down. Colour scale is colour-blind safe and has a table equivalent.

F-64Access and outcome gap Should

The hardest and most important chart: estimated years of healthy life lost and undiagnosed or missing people by country. Handle with visible methodology and no dramatisation.

F-65Technology uptake Should

Share of registry populations using CGM, pumps and AID over time, by age band, where registries publish it.

F-66Outcome distributions Should

HbA1c and time-in-range distributions by registry and age band, as distributions rather than single means.

F-67Compare countries Should

Pin up to five countries onto one time series with a shareable URL.

F-68Export and cite Must

Every chart offers PNG, the underlying CSV, and a formatted citation for the original dataset — pointing at the publisher, not at InRange.

F-69Methodology panel Must

Per chart: what is being counted, who collected it, known gaps, why countries differ, and what the numbers cannot tell you. Written once per chart, non-negotiable.

M6

Search & discovery

Week 7

F-70Global search Must

One box across stories, news items and devices using Postgres full-text search with trigram fallback for typos. Results grouped by type with per-type filters.

  • Search returns in under 200 ms at 100k items
  • Ranking is deterministic and explainable, not a black box

F-71Topic hubs Should

A landing page per major tag combining stories, news and devices — the main SEO surface and the natural entry point for a searching parent.

F-72Related content Could

Tag-overlap recommendations at the foot of every page. No personalisation.

M7

Moderation & trust

Weeks 1 and 7

F-73Moderation console Must

Unified queue for reports, first-time author posts, comments from new accounts and correction requests. Actions: approve, hide, unpublish, warn, suspend. Every action logged with actor, reason and timestamp.

F-74Abuse controls Must

Per-IP and per-account rate limits, new-account posting throttles, link caps, a domain blocklist, and honeypot fields on public forms.

F-75Safety interstitial Must

Any content touching dosing, unproven cures, or crisis topics carries a standing notice that InRange is not medical advice, with links to the person's own care team and recognised support organisations.

  • Notice appears on the page, not only in the footer
  • Editorial policy page states what will be removed and why

F-76In-app notifications Could

Replies, approvals, moderation outcomes. In-app only; email notifications are post-MVP.

M8

Platform & compliance

Weeks 1 and 8

F-80SEO and structured data Must

Server-rendered public pages, sitemaps per content type, Open Graph images generated at the edge, and JSON-LD for Article, Dataset and FAQ.

F-81Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA Must

Keyboard paths for the editor and the moderation console, contrast checked against the palette, chart data available as a table, motion reduced on request.

  • Every chart has a "view as table" control
  • Editor is fully operable without a mouse

F-82Privacy and GDPR posture Must

EU hosting, cookieless analytics, no third-party trackers, a records-of-processing entry, a data-protection notice written in plain language, and no special-category data collected beyond what a user volunteers in their own prose.

F-83Observability Must

Structured JSON logs with request IDs, traces on GraphQL resolvers and ingestion runs, error reporting, uptime checks and a status page.

F-84Static page editor Should

Admin-editable Markdown for About, Editorial policy, Safety, Privacy and Contact so copy changes don't need a deploy.

F-85Backups and restore drill Must

Nightly Postgres backups with point-in-time recovery, plus one rehearsed restore into a scratch environment before launch. An untested backup is not a backup.

Eight one-week sprints

Roadmap

Each week ends with something demonstrable in a browser. If a week's exit criteria are not met by Friday, the following week's Should items are cut rather than the week being extended — the schedule is fixed, the scope moves.

Standing ruleDeploy to production from week one. A week that ends only on localhost has not ended.
01

Foundations and identity

Nothing user-facing except sign-up, but every later week depends on this being right. Resist the urge to start on stories.

Backend
  • Cargo workspace, axum + async-graphql skeleton
  • Postgres, sqlx migrations, connection pool
  • Config, typed errors, request IDs
  • Argon2id auth, sessions, roles (F-01 → F-05, F-07)
  • Tracing, health endpoints (F-83)
Frontend
  • Next.js App Router, TypeScript strict
  • Tailwind theme from the design tokens
  • GraphQL codegen wired to the Rust schema
  • Auth pages, layout shell, nav
  • Safety notice and editorial policy pages (F-75)
Ops
  • GitHub Actions: fmt, clippy, test, build
  • Docker images for API and web
  • EU-region host, managed Postgres, object storage
  • Staging and production environments
Exit criteriaA new account can be created on the production URL, verify its email, sign in, edit a profile and sign out everywhere. CI blocks a merge on a clippy warning.
02

Stories

The first surface anyone can actually use. Writing must feel calm and safe, because the people using it are describing the worst weeks of their lives.

Backend

  • Server-side Markdown sanitising
  • Upload endpoint, resize, WebP, EXIF strip (F-11)

Frontend

Content

  • Draft the contributor guidelines

03

Ingestion engine above range

The hardest week. Every source misbehaves differently. Budget the whole week for the pipeline and none of it for UI, and have the cut list ready before Monday.

Worker
  • Scheduler, job table, per-host limiter (F-31)
  • Conditional GET with ETag and Last-Modified
  • Generic RSS/Atom/JSON Feed adapter (F-32)
  • API adapters: PubMed, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA, Crossref, GitHub releases
  • Canonical URL, DOI/PMID/NCT identity, SimHash dedup (F-33)
  • Rule-based classifier plus embedding fallback (F-34)
Backend
  • Source registry and admin mutations (F-30)
  • Item, mention and entity tables (F-35)
  • Fetch-run metrics and health view (F-43)
Cut list, prepared in advance
  • Drop embedding classification, ship rules only
  • Drop Crossref and GitHub adapters to week 4
  • Drop entity tagging (F-35) entirely
Exit criteriaThirty sources poll on schedule, a syndicated story collapses into one item with several mentions, an intentionally broken source auto-pauses and appears in the health view, and a second run of the same feed writes nothing new.

Where Signal gets its material

News sources

Sources are grouped by tier, because trust tier drives both the editorial badge a reader sees and how aggressively the classifier trusts the item. Start with roughly 30 sources: enough for a full feed, few enough to fix by hand when they break.

Before you add any sourceConfirm the current feed or API endpoint at build time and record its terms of use in the registry row. Publishers move feeds and change terms; treat any URL written in a document as a hint, not a fact. The discovery routine in the backend spec resolves feeds from a site's <link rel="alternate"> tags automatically.

Tier 1 — Primary research and registries

Licensing posture, applied to every tierStore the headline, up to 240 characters of excerpt, the publication date, the source name and the canonical URL — nothing more. This is both the legal position and the reason publishers will tolerate you.
Method

How news, studies and data are collected

Nine stages, in order. Each stage is a separate, testable unit in the worker, and each writes a record so a failure can be traced to a specific item and a specific run.

STAGE 01

Discover

Given a homepage, resolve feeds from link rel="alternate", then try conventional paths, then check for a documented API. Discovery is run once when a source is added and re-run monthly to catch moved feeds.

STAGE 02

Fetch

Conditional GET with stored ETag and Last-Modified, a five-second connect timeout, per-host concurrency of one, jittered scheduling, exponential backoff, and a User-Agent naming the project with a contact URL. Scraped sources check robots.txt first.

STAGE 03

Parse

One parser for RSS, Atom and JSON Feed; typed adapters for each API. Adapters emit the same normalised struct, so everything downstream is source-agnostic.

STAGE 04

Normalise

Canonicalise the URL (strip UTM and click identifiers, resolve redirects, lower-case the host, drop trailing slashes), coerce dates to UTC, decode entities, collapse whitespace, and truncate the excerpt to the licensing limit at this stage rather than at render time.

STAGE 05

Identify and deduplicate

Strong identity first: DOI, PMID, NCT number, or 510(k) number. Failing that, a SimHash over the title and first sentence within a 72-hour window. Matches become mentions on an existing item rather than new items.

STAGE 06

Classify

Keyword and pattern rules assign a category and confidence. Anything below the confidence floor is embedded and compared to category centroids. Anything still ambiguous goes to the editor marked unclassified. Rules are data, editable in admin.

STAGE 07

Enrich

Attach evidence metadata for research items (design, phase, participant count, registry ID, peer-review status), link manufacturers and devices by alias table, and resolve the journal from Crossref.

STAGE 08

Score

A transparent, tunable score: source trust tier, recency, evidence strength, entity match to tracked devices, and corroboration count. Score orders the editorial queue — it never publishes anything.

STAGE 09

Review and publish

An editor approves, rewrites the summary in their own words, adjusts tags and publishes. Nothing is auto-published in the MVP. Every action is written to the audit log.

Study and dataset collection

Research is collected differently from news, because the unit is a study rather than an article.

Standing queries

Each research source has a saved query stored in the registry — condition terms crossed with technology, immunology, screening and outcome terms — versioned, so you can tell whether a spike is real or a query change.

Study-level identity

A trial is followed across registration, results posting, preprint, publication and press release. All five attach to one study record, which is what lets the item page show a trial's full arc.

Evidence classification

Design (RCT, observational, registry analysis, case series, in vitro, animal), phase, participant count, and publication status. This drives the badges that stop a mouse study being read as a cure.

Human summarisation

Two sentences: what was done, and what it does and does not show. An LLM may draft it; an editor always rewrites and approves it. The draft is stored separately from the published summary so the two can be compared.

Statistics collection

Slower, manual, versioned. These datasets update annually, not hourly — resist automating what happens once a year.

Manual acquisition, scripted transformation

Download the published file by hand, commit it to a raw store with its retrieval date and checksum, then transform it with a script that is committed alongside. Re-running the script must reproduce the table exactly.

Harmonisation

ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 country codes, a fixed set of age bands with an explicit mapping table per source, and both counts and rates stored where the source publishes them — never derived silently.

Provenance to the cell

Every observation row carries its dataset version and source row. A reader clicking a bar can be shown exactly where that number came from.

Licence gate

A dataset without a recorded licence and permitted-use note cannot be attached to a chart. This is enforced by a foreign key, not by discipline.

Operating costRealistically this pipeline needs about 20 minutes a day of editorial attention once tuned, plus an hour a month fixing sources that changed. Budget it as ongoing work, not as a one-off build.
The Numbers surface

Interactive statistics

The goal is not a dashboard. It is to let someone answer a specific question — how many children are diagnosed each year where I live, is that rising, how does access here compare — and understand how much confidence the answer deserves.

Candidate datasets

International Diabetes Federation — Diabetes Atlas

Global prevalence and incidence estimates by country, including under-20 type 1 figures. Broad coverage, modelled for many countries — say so.

T1D Index

Country-level modelled estimates of prevalence, missing people, and healthy years lost. The most communicative dataset in the field and the most in need of a methodology panel.

CDC diabetes surveillance and SEARCH for Diabetes in Youth

US incidence and prevalence in youth by age, sex and race or ethnicity, with real trend data.

National registries

Sweden's NDR, the DPV registry in Germany and Austria, Scotland's SCI-Diabetes, and T1D Exchange QI. These are the only credible sources for technology uptake and outcome distributions.

WHO Global Health Observatory

Context indicators — mortality, health expenditure, insulin availability — for the access story.

Check each licence before use; several permit reuse with attribution, some restrict redistribution of the underlying file. The registry field is there to record the answer, and the chart cannot ship without it.

The charts

Incidence over time Must

New diagnoses per 100,000 per year, filtered by country and age band, with a note wherever a definition or method changed mid-series.

Prevalence map Must

Choropleth with a year scrubber, a colour-blind-safe sequential scale, and a country drill-down panel.

Compare countries Should

Up to five countries on one series, shareable by URL, with modelled estimates rendered in a visibly different line style from measured data.

Technology uptake Should

Share of a registry population on CGM, pump or AID over time, by age band. The clearest evidence that the field is changing.

Outcome distributions Should

HbA1c and time-in-range shown as distributions with medians and interquartile ranges, never as a single national average.

Access gap Should

Estimated missing people and healthy years lost by country. Requires the most careful copy of anything on the platform.

Rules that apply to every chartState the source, the version and the retrieval date on the chart itself. Distinguish measured from modelled visually, not only in a footnote. Never interpolate across a gap without marking it. Offer the underlying numbers as a table and as CSV. Cite the original publisher, not InRange. And write, in plain language, what the chart cannot tell you.
